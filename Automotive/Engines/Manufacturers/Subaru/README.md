# Subaru Engine Database

## Overview

Subaru Corporation (formerly Fuji Heavy Industries) has been building vehicles since 1953 and automobiles since 1958. Known for their signature horizontally-opposed "boxer" engines, symmetrical all-wheel drive systems, and rally heritage, Subaru has carved a unique niche in the automotive world.

**Headquarters:** Ota, Gunma, Japan
**Founded:** 1953 (as Fuji Heavy Industries), renamed Subaru Corporation in 2017
**Parent:** Toyota Motor Corporation (20% stake since 2019)

## Historical Timeline

### Origins (1953-1958)
- **1953**: Fuji Heavy Industries formed from five companies previously part of Nakajima Aircraft
- **1954**: First vehicle - Subaru 1500 (prototype, limited production)
- **1958**: Subaru 360 "Ladybug" - Japan's first mass-market microcar

### The Kei Car Era (1958-1970)
- **EK31/EK32**: 356cc 2-cylinder air-cooled engines powered the iconic Subaru 360
- Focus on affordable, fuel-efficient microcars for post-war Japan
- Over 392,000 Subaru 360s produced

### Birth of the Boxer (1966-1979)
- **1966**: EA52 - Subaru's first horizontally-opposed four-cylinder engine
- **Subaru 1000**: First front-wheel-drive car from a Japanese manufacturer
- **EA63/EA71**: Evolution of the water-cooled boxer platform
- Introduction of 4WD with the Subaru Leone (1972)

### Performance Emergence (1979-1989)
- **EA81/EA82**: Larger displacement boxers with turbo options
- **1983**: First turbocharged Subaru engine (EA82T)
- Rally program beginnings in the World Rally Championship

### The EJ Era - Golden Age (1989-2021)
- **EJ20**: Legendary 2.0L boxer powering everything from economy cars to WRC champions
- **1992**: First WRX (World Rally eXperimental) in Japan
- **1994**: Impreza WRX STI with EJ20G
- **2004**: EJ257 - The iconic 2.5L STI powerplant
- **3 WRC Manufacturers' Championships** (1995, 1996, 1997)
- **3 WRC Drivers' Championships** (Colin McRae 1995, Richard Burns 2001, Petter Solberg 2003)

### Modern Era (2010-Present)
- **FA/FB Series**: New generation boxers with improved efficiency
- **FA20**: Joint development with Toyota for BRZ/86 sports car
- **FA24**: 2.4L turbo for new-generation WRX
- Focus on safety (EyeSight) and hybrid technology

## Subaru Engine Characteristics

### The Boxer Advantage
1. **Lower Center of Gravity**: Flat configuration drops CoG by ~50mm vs inline engines
2. **Symmetrical Layout**: Perfect for AWD weight distribution
3. **Vibration Characteristics**: Primary balance inherent (second-order vibrations remain)
4. **Compact Length**: Short crankshaft = high rigidity
5. **Crash Safety**: Engine slides under cabin in frontal collision

### Common Traits Across All Boxers
- **Firing Order**: 1-3-2-4 (EJ), 1-3-4-2 (FA/FB)
- **Timing System**: Belts (EJ) or Chains (FA/FB)
- **Head Gasket Issues**: Notorious on EJ25 NA engines
- **AWD Integration**: Designed from ground up for symmetrical AWD

## Common Issues by Era

### EA Series (1966-1994)
- Oil consumption in high-mileage examples
- Carburetor issues on early models
- Limited parts availability for restoration

### EJ Series (1989-2021)
- **EJ25 NA**: Head gasket failure (coolant-to-oil leak)
- **EJ20/EJ25 Turbo**: Ringland failure under knock
- **All EJ**: Timing belt maintenance critical (every 105k miles)
- **EJ257**: Prone to spun rod bearings above 400hp

### EZ Series (1999-2019)
- Oil consumption in early EZ30
- Timing chain guide wear
- More reliable than EJ series overall

### FA/FB Series (2010-Present)
- **FA20**: Oil starvation concerns, valve spring recalls
- **FB**: Generally reliable, some oil consumption
- **FA24**: Too new for long-term data, strong initial reliability
