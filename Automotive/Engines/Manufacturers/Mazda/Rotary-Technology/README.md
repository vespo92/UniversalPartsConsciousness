# Mazda Rotary Engine Technology - The Ultimate Anomaly

## When Everyone Went Left, Mazda Went Right

This is the definitive documentation of the most stubborn engineering decision in automotive history.

In the 1960s, every major automaker invested in Wankel rotary technology. By the late 1970s, every single one had abandoned it.

**Except Mazda.**

For over 50 years, a small company from Hiroshima, Japan, persisted with an engine design the entire automotive industry declared dead. They refined it. They raced it. They won Le Mans with it. They proved the critics wrong.

## Key Achievements

### 1991 Le Mans 24 Hours - OVERALL VICTORY
- **Car:** Mazda 787B
- **Engine:** R26B 4-rotor, 700 HP, 10,500 RPM
- **Significance:** Only rotary engine to ever win Le Mans
- **Aftermath:** Regulations changed to effectively ban rotary

### Production Milestones
- **2,000,000+** rotary vehicles sold
- **811,634** RX-7s produced
- **192,094** RX-8s produced
- **50+** years of continuous rotary development

## The Rituals

Rotary ownership requires understanding and participation:

1. **The Warmup** - Never shut off a cold engine
2. **The Cooldown** - Idle before shutdown
3. **The Redline Ritual** - Weekly high-RPM runs
4. **The Premix** - Adding 2-stroke oil to fuel
5. **The Dipstick Check** - Oil level at every fill-up

## The Sound

No engine sounds like a rotary. The high-pitched scream at 8000 RPM, the rapid-fire exhaust pulses, the distinctive "brap-brap-brap" at idle - all unmistakable.

The 787B at Le Mans could be identified by sound alone, even among V12s and turbocharged piston engines.
